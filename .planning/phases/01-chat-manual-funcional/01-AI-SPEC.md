## 1b. Domain Context

> Researched by `gsd-domain-researcher`. Grounds the evaluation strategy in domain expert knowledge.

**Industry Vertical:** Used automotive / seminovos marketplace (Brazil) — private individual seller (Pessoa Física) transactions

**User Population:** Felipe Grunewald (AutoAgent founder/operator) uses this tool live during validation interviews with lojistas (dealership owners) and investors. He opens a real vehicle listing, enters the data, and runs a live negotiation in front of an audience who is evaluating whether AI can plausibly do this job. Lucas (implementer) also uses it in isolation for prompt calibration and batch testing.

**Stakes Level:** High — each negotiation session during a demo is a live business case. Poor AI performance (robotic tone, random concessions, wrong Portuguese register, price capitulation) directly invalidates the argument that AutoAgent's core engine works. There is no "offline" consequence window; the failure is visible in real time to the people whose buy-in is being sought.

**Output Consequence:** If the AI agent fails to close at 20–30% below FIPE convincingly — or sounds obviously artificial — the AutoAgent business model is invalidated in the room. The downstream effect is: no lojista partnership, no investor interest, and no justification to build the full product. Conversely, a clean negotiation that closes 20–25% below FIPE in 4–6 rounds is direct evidence that the core technical hypothesis holds.

### What Domain Experts Evaluate Against

Domain context: Brazil's seminovos market has well-established bargaining norms. PF sellers on OLX/WebMotors expect negotiation — price reductions of 5–15% are routine, and sellers know buyers will probe. An experienced buyer (or AutoAgent professional) reads the negotiation transcript and evaluates the following:

| Dimension | Good (expert accepts) | Bad (expert flags) | Stakes | Source |
|-----------|----------------------|-------------------|--------|--------|
| **Anchor credibility** | Agent opens with a price clearly derived from FIPE, citing the reference explicitly (e.g., "A FIPE para esse modelo está em R$ X, e comparáveis da região saíram a R$ Y…"). Anchor is 25–35% below ask price — aggressive but defensible. | Agent opens with a round number that has no stated basis, or anchors so low it reads as bad faith and shuts the conversation down, or opens at or near ask price (signals no intent to negotiate). | Critical — the opening offer sets the entire negotiation range. An indefensible anchor destroys trust on message 1. | [ASSUMED] Brazilian automotive broker practice; PROJECT-BRIEF section 9.1 anchor tactic |
| **Concession logic** | Each price increase is explicitly tied to a stated reason (e.g., "dado que o carro tem revisões em dia e você está disposto a aguardar a vistoria, consigo subir para R$ X"). Steps are small (R$ 1k–3k per round per brief). The agent never skips steps. | Agent raises price without explanation ("posso oferecer R$ X agora"), raises in large irregular jumps, or lowers a previously stated offer (signals no internal consistency — a real buyer would not do this). | High — concession pattern is how a trained eye detects whether the agent is running a real strategy or responding randomly to the last message. | [ASSUMED] Negotiation theory (anchoring + incremental concession); PROJECT-BRIEF section 9.1 tactic 2 |
| **Argument quality** | Arguments are grounded in vehicle-specific facts present in the listing: km, days on market, number of price reductions, city/region comparables. Agent cites at least one data point per round. Example: "Esse modelo com mais de 80 mil km está saindo a R$ X na região — encontrei 11 comparáveis recentes." | Agent uses generic arguments ("o mercado está difícil", "preços caíram") without specifics. Agent ignores the km or days-online data that was provided. Agent invents comparables not in the system prompt context. | High — a PF seller who has been negotiating their car for weeks will immediately detect a buyer who has not actually looked at the market. Vague arguments read as bluffing. | [ASSUMED] Seminovos market dynamics; PROJECT-BRIEF section 9.1 tactic 4 + anti-tactic (no lying about comparables) |
| **Hard stop discipline** | Agent never accepts a price above `walkAwayPrice`. When maxRounds is reached without closing, agent delivers a clean exit: states final offer, thanks the seller, leaves the door open. Encerramento is graceful, not abrupt. | Agent accepts a price above target ("ok, fechamos por R$ X" where X > walkAwayPrice). Agent keeps sending messages after round limit is exceeded. Agent's exit sounds frustrated or threatening. | Critical — exceeding targetPrice is the definition of mission failure. It also breaks the live demo narrative entirely. | PROJECT-BRIEF section 9.1 HARD STOPS |
| **Tone in pt-BR** | Professional but warm, in the register of a competent intermediador (not a street market bargainer, not a corporate lawyer). Uses "você" consistently. WhatsApp-like sentence length (2–3 short paragraphs). No gírias. Emojis only on close. Acknowledges the seller's perspective before pivoting ("Entendo que o senhor investiu muito no carro, e de fato ele está em ótimo estado…"). | Uses "tu" (wrong register for most of Brazil outside RS/RJ). Uses formal third person ("o senhor") when casual is appropriate. Sounds like translated English ("no entanto, gostaríamos de propor"). Sounds robotic (bullet points, numbered lists, headers in a chat message). Aggressive or dismissive ("esse preço está muito fora do mercado"). | High — tone is the primary signal that distinguishes a human intermediador from an obvious bot. This dimension matters most for the "lojistas trust AI negotiator" hypothesis (H3). | [ASSUMED] Brazilian Portuguese sociolinguistics; PROJECT-BRIEF section 9.1 TOM |
| **Close recognition** | When seller signals acceptance proximity (e.g., "vou pensar", "você consegue chegar em R$ X?", "o que você acha de X?"), agent does not open a new round of argument — it moves to close: confirms the number, states next steps (vistoria, contrato de exclusividade, pagamento em 48h), creates forward motion. | Agent ignores a soft-close signal and continues with another round of justification, re-opening price room the seller was about to concede. Or agent asks clarifying questions when the seller has signaled readiness. | High — failing to close when the seller is ready wastes the round budget and can cause the seller to reconsider. In a live demo, it makes the agent look like it does not understand human communication. | [ASSUMED] Negotiation closing theory; PROJECT-BRIEF mockChatHistories[1] closing pattern |
| **Resistance handling** | Agent has prepared responses to the three most common objections in the Brazilian PF seminovos market: (1) "o preço é justo" / "não está tão abaixo do mercado" — counter with FIPE delta and days-on-market data; (2) "não tenho pressa, espero o preço certo" — acknowledge patience, introduce opportunity cost of holding (insurance, IPVA, depreciation curve); (3) "tem outro interessado" — do not capitulate, validate interest without urgency panic, hold position with small goodwill concession. | Agent capitulates immediately on any objection ("entendo, podemos chegar a R$ X então"). Agent ignores the objection and repeats prior argument verbatim. Agent calls the seller's bluff aggressively ("isso é uma técnica comum de negociação"). | High — resistance is the core of every negotiation. An agent that cannot handle standard objections fails the "convincing with real PFs" test immediately. | [ASSUMED] Brazilian automotive PF seller behavior patterns |

### Known Failure Modes in This Domain

These are domain-specific failure modes — not generic AI hallucination, but how it manifests in a seminovos negotiation context:

1. **Random concession without justification.** Agent raises its offer without explaining why (e.g., responds to "não posso" with "tudo bem, então ofereço R$ X"). This is the single most damaging failure: it signals the agent has no strategy and will simply cave if pressured. A real buyer never raises without a stated reason. Every price increase must come with a named trigger.

2. **Price capitulation above targetPrice.** Agent accepts a final price above `walkAwayPrice`. This is a hard business failure, not a tone issue. Caused by: system prompt not asserting the hard stop clearly enough, or the agent being "helpful" in a way that overrides the constraint. Must be enforced at both prompt and code level (post-process check on extracted final price).

3. **Wrong Portuguese register.** Using "tu" instead of "você", overly formal language ("prezado senhor"), or translated-English phrasing ("no entanto, gostaria de propor") immediately signals non-native writing. Brazilian sellers detect this and disengage. The system prompt must set register explicitly and the few-shot must demonstrate it consistently.

4. **Ignoring seller signals in the listing data.** Agent sends a generic negotiation message that would work for any car, ignoring the km, days-online, and price-reduction data that was explicitly provided. This fails the argument quality dimension and makes the agent look like it did not read the listing. Every message should reference at least one data point from the anúncio.

5. **Negotiating past maxRounds without closing.** Agent reaches round 6 and keeps offering new arguments instead of executing the exit protocol. In a live demo, this makes the tool appear broken. The round counter must be a hard constraint with a scripted exit sequence.

6. **Revealing the discount strategy or target price.** Agent explicitly tells the seller "estou tentando fechar 25% abaixo da FIPE" or references the internal `targetPrice` variable in its message. This destroys the plausibility of the intermediador persona and exposes the playbook. The system prompt must prohibit this explicitly; the few-shot must show the agent staying in character.

7. **Markdown bleed into chat messages.** Agent produces messages with `**bold**`, bullet lists, or headers — standard LLM output format that looks completely wrong in a WhatsApp-style chat bubble. The system prompt must instruct "plain text only, no markdown" and this should be tested explicitly in evals.

8. **Opener too aggressive for the context.** If the car has been listed only 2 days and has zero price reductions, opening 30% below ask may trigger an immediate rejection and end the conversation. The agent must modulate anchor aggressiveness based on seller motivation signals — a feature of Phase 2 intelligence but the Phase 1 prompt should at least not hardcode a single anchor depth.

### Regulatory / Compliance Context

Not regulated. This is an internal demo tool with no PII storage, no financial transactions, and no third-party data exposure. LGPD does not apply (no personal data is collected, stored, or processed beyond the session). BACEN/SUSEP intermediation regulations do not apply (no actual purchase transaction occurs). No compliance review required before deployment.

Note for future phases: if the tool evolves to contact real PF sellers via WhatsApp, LGPD consent requirements and BACEN intermediation rules would apply. That is out of scope for this playground.
